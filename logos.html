Tech logos, done properly.

Six new directions, grounded in what's actually working in 2026 tech branding — Vercel, Linear, Anthropic, Cursor, Raycast. Geometric marks, negative-space play, monochrome cores, one accent. Forget literal letterforms; these are abstract symbols a tech company can own.

ResearchWhat the best tech brands of 2026 do

Neo-minimalism with personality. Clean geometric marks, but with one moment of warmth — a curve, a negative-space hint, a single accent color. Vercel's triangle, Anthropic's terracotta asterisk, Linear's L-rectangles. Negative space tells the story. The best 2026 marks reward a second look. Monochrome cores with one bold accent. Black/white as the structure, one warm or electric color for character. Animation-ready — built so they can pulse, rotate, or assemble in interfaces.

Aperture

01 / Negative-space

Two squares overlapping at 45° create an eight-point star with a precision-cut center. The negative space is the story — the “edge” lives in what's missing. Think Vercel's triangle, but for a studio that builds at the intersection of disciplines.

Aperture — in coral.

Most distinctive of the six. Negative-space mark that tells a story, scales from favicon to billboard, and is animation-ready (rotate the squares 22.5° on hover and you have a brand moment).

Construction

Two 120×120 squares centered on a 200×200 canvas. One rotated 45°. The center counter is a 20-radius circle in paper. Margins are 40 units on every side. Three numbers, one mark.

Why this one

Distinctive. No other tech company owns an 8-point aperture mark — it's space we can claim cleanly.

Symbolic. The negative star at the center is the “edge” — the meeting point. We sell what happens at that meeting.

Animatable. Hover the logo on the site and the inner square rotates 22.5° to dilate the aperture wider — a literal brand moment, repeatable across product UI.

Resilient. Reads at 16px favicon and on a billboard. Works reversed. Works in monochrome. Works without the accent dot.
